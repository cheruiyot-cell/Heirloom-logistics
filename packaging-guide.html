Moving Day Is Chaotic. Your First Night Doesn't Have to Be.

You have packed the boxes. You have booked the movers. You have coordinated the logistics. The last thing you want is to arrive at your new home, exhausted and hungry, only to realize that your toothbrush is buried in a box somewhere and you have no idea where the bedsheets are.

This guide is designed to help you prepare a "First Night Box" — a small collection of essentials that will carry you through your first 24 hours in your new home. Pack this box last, keep it with you during the move (not in the truck), and unpack it first when you arrive.

The "First Night Box" Checklist

Keep this box with you in your car or at the front of the truck so you can access it immediately.

🛏️ Bedtime Essentials

Why: After a long day of moving, you want to collapse into a made bed.

🚿 Toiletries

Why: A hot shower after moving day feels like heaven.

👕 Clothing for the Next Day

Why: You may not have time to unpack your wardrobe immediately.

🍽️ Kitchen & Food

Why: You will be hungry and thirsty. Easy access makes it feel less like camping.

📱 Electronics & Chargers

Why: Your phone is your lifeline on moving day.

📋 Important Documents & Valuables

Why: These should never go in the moving truck.

🧹 Cleaning Supplies

Why: A quick clean makes the space feel more welcoming.

The "Pack Last, Unpack First" Strategy

📦 Pack Last

  • The First Night Box
  • Everyday cooking items
  • A small toolkit
  • Chargers and electronics

📤 Unpack First

  • Bedroom essentials
  • Bathroom essentials
  • Kitchen basics
  • Clothing for the next day

Everything else can wait. You do not need to unpack the entire house on day one.

Items You Should NOT Pack in the Moving Truck

⚠️

Hazardous Materials

Petrol, propane tanks, paint thinners, matches, lighters, fireworks. These are a fire risk.

🥬

Perishable Food

Fresh produce, dairy, and frozen items. Eat them before the move or give them away.

🌱

Plants

Most moving trucks are not climate-controlled. Move them in your car if possible.

📄

Personal Documents

Passports, IDs, title deeds, certificates. Keep these with you at all times.

💍

Jewelry & Cash

These small valuables are too risky to put in a truck. Keep them on your person.

💊

Medications

If you need them daily, keep them with you. Don't pack them in a box that might get buried.

The Labeling System That Saves Hours

The biggest mistake people make when packing is writing vague labels like "Kitchen Stuff" or "Miscellaneous." Here is the Heirloom Logistics labeling system:

The Three-Part Label:

1. Room Name: Kitchen, Bedroom 1, Living Room

2. Contents Summary: Pots, pans, utensils

3. Priority Level: HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW

Example Label:

KITCHEN

Pots, pans, utensils

PRIORITY: HIGH

Pro Tip: Write the label on the side of the box, not the top. When boxes are stacked, you can still read the labels.

Seven Tips to Make Moving Day Easier

1

Start Early

The earlier you start, the more daylight you have for unloading and settling in.

2

Dress Comfortably

Wear comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes. You will be on your feet all day.

3

Keep Snacks and Water Handy

You will get hungry and dehydrated. Keep a small cooler with water and easy snacks.

4

Keep Your Phone Charged

A dead phone is a disaster on moving day. Keep your charger accessible.

5

Take Photos of Electronics

Before you unplug your TV or computer, take a photo of the back so you remember which cable goes where.

6

Do a Final Walkthrough

Before the truck leaves, walk through every room, open every cupboard, and check every drawer.

7

Breathe

Moving is stressful, but it is also exciting. Take a moment to appreciate the new chapter.
